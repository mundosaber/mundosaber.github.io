Nano Banana Pro Prompts: 12 Copy-Paste Prompts That Actually Work (November 2026)

By Mark Smith  ·  23 November 2026  ·  AI Prompts

Most Nano Banana prompt lists are still the same old figurine prompts written for the original model. They work — but they don't use what Pro is actually good at: keeping faces and people consistent across edits, rendering readable text on posters and infographics, and blending up to 14 reference images into one scene. These 12 prompts are written for Nano Banana Pro, using Google's own prompting formula, so you can paste them and get the kind of result the prompt describes.

What Nano Banana Pro is: Google launched it on 20 November 2025 as Gemini 3 Pro Image, built on Gemini 3 Pro. In the Gemini app you use it by selecting "Create images" with the "Thinking" model. The free tier of the Gemini app uses the faster Nano Banana 2 model instead; paid Google AI plans add a "Redo with Pro" option. Pro can blend up to 14 reference images, keep the resemblance of up to five people, and output at 1K, 2K or 4K. Every image Gemini makes carries an invisible SynthID watermark, and images from the Gemini app also carry a visible watermark.

Before you paste anything: Google's help pages say you must be 18 or over to edit images in Gemini. And Nano Banana redraws your photo rather than retouching it — anything you don't pin down can drift. That is why every photo-edit prompt below starts by telling it exactly what must stay the same.

The Formula Behind Every Prompt Below

Google's Cloud prompting guide for the Nano Banana models boils a good prompt down to five parts, in this order: [Subject] + [Action] + [Location / context] + [Composition] + [Style]. Google's developer docs add the same habits: be hyper-specific about materials, colours and shapes, state the purpose ("a logo for a minimalist skincare brand" beats "a logo"), and put any words to render inside quotation marks. Every prompt below follows that shape — fill in the [brackets], paste it with your photo where one is needed, and change one thing at a time if the first result isn't right.

How to Use These Prompts

  1. Open the Gemini app or gemini.google.com and pick Create images from the tools menu.
  2. For Pro, select the Thinking model first; on a paid Google AI plan you can also generate with Nano Banana 2 and choose Redo with Pro.
  3. Upload your photo where the prompt says "uploaded photo" — for photo edits, use a clear, front-lit photo where the face is large and in focus.
  4. Paste the whole prompt, replace everything in [brackets], and send it.
  5. If something is wrong, fix only that one thing in a follow-up ("Keep everything the same. Change only the background to a beach at sunset.") instead of starting over.

Photo Edits That Keep Your Face

Upload your photo with each of these. The first sentence is the same in all four — it is what stops the face from drifting.

1. 1980s studio portrait

Use the uploaded photo as the reference and keep the person's face, facial features, skin tone and expression exactly the same. Turn it into a 1980s portrait studio photo: a blue and pink laser-beam backdrop, a soft-focus glow, big voluminous hair, and a bright windbreaker or a blazer with shoulder pads. Light the face with a direct front flash and add a coloured back light. Finish with warm film grain and the slightly faded colours of an 80s print. Head and shoulders, vertical.

2. Professional headshot

Use the uploaded photo as the reference and keep the person's face, facial features, skin tone and expression exactly the same. Turn it into a professional LinkedIn headshot: a plain light-grey studio background, a dark blazer over a plain shirt, shoulders square to the camera, soft even studio light with no harsh shadows. Photorealistic, natural skin texture, sharp eyes, head and shoulders, square format.

3. 1990s film photo

Use the uploaded photo as the reference and keep the person's face, facial features, skin tone and expression exactly the same. Turn it into a candid 1990s film photograph: warm faded colours, subtle film grain, soft nostalgic daylight, the look of a disposable camera photo taken outdoors with friends. Do not add or remove anyone. Photorealistic, natural skin texture.

4. New background, same person

Use the uploaded photo as the reference and keep the person's face, facial features, skin tone, hair and outfit exactly the same. Change only the background to [a sunny seaside promenade at golden hour]. Match the light on the face to the new scene. Photorealistic, natural skin texture, sharp eyes, natural hands. No text, no logos.

Posters, Text and Infographics

This is where Pro earns its keep — Google says it is its best model for correctly rendered, legible text. Put the exact words in quotation marks and say where they go.

5. Event poster with readable text

Create a poster for [a neighbourhood bake sale on Saturday 12 December] with the text "[Fresh Bakes, Warm Hearts]" in large bold letters at the top and "[Saturday 12 December, 10am, Community Hall]" in smaller letters at the bottom. Warm bakery scene in the middle: a wooden table with bread, cakes and jam jars, morning light through a window. Cozy, friendly style, warm colours. Spell every word exactly as written.

6. Infographic from your facts

Create a clean infographic titled "[How to Water Houseplants]" with exactly three steps: "1. Check the soil", "2. Water slowly", "3. Let it drain". One small simple icon beside each step, a white background, dark green headings, plenty of empty space. Spell every word exactly as written. No extra text, no logos.

7. Book-cover style image

Create a book cover for a novel called "[The Last Lighthouse]" with the title in large elegant serif letters across the top third and the author name "[Your Name]" in small letters at the bottom. A lone lighthouse on a rocky coast at dusk, dramatic clouds, deep blue and gold palette. Moody, cinematic, no other text.

Product Shots

8. Studio product photo

A high-resolution, studio-lit product photograph of [a matte black ceramic coffee mug] on a light wooden table. The lighting is a three-point softbox setup to show the mug's shape and matte texture. The camera angle is a slight low angle to showcase the handle. Ultra-realistic, with sharp focus on the mug's rim. No text, no logos, no hands.

9. Product photo from your own picture

Use the uploaded photo of [my handmade soap bars] as the exact reference. Keep the product's shape, colours, label and proportions exactly as in the photo. Place it on a clean white stone surface with a small green leaf beside it, soft daylight from the left, gentle shadow. Studio product photography, square format. No added text, no extra products.

Multi-Image and Character Prompts

Pro's signature trick is blending several reference images — up to 14, and up to five people kept consistent. Upload all the photos the prompt mentions.

10. Same character, new scene

Using the uploaded character photo as the reference, show the same person in a new scene: [reading on a park bench in autumn]. Keep the face shape, hair, clothing and colour palette identical to the reference. The person is sitting relaxed with an open book, seen from a three-quarter angle. Style: photorealistic, soft afternoon light, fallen leaves. No text.

11. Two people, one photo

Use both uploaded photos as references. Keep each person's face, facial features, skin tone and body shape exactly as in their own photo, and show only these two people. Put them side by side at a [Christmas market at dusk], smiling at the camera, in warm winter coats. Fairy lights and wooden stalls behind them, soft evening light. Photorealistic, natural skin texture. No text.

12. Collectible figurine

Create a 1/7 scale commercialised figurine of the person in the uploaded photo, in a realistic style, in a real environment. The figurine is placed on a computer desk with a round transparent acrylic base with no text on the base. The computer screen behind it shows the 3D modelling process of this figurine. Next to the screen is the toy packaging box, printed with original artwork of the character. Keep the person's face and likeness exactly the same.

If the face still changes: the usual cause is a vague prompt — the model redraws whatever you didn't pin down. Add the keep-list back in ("Keep the face, hair, skin tone and expression exactly the same"), use a clearer front-lit photo, and change one thing per try instead of rewriting the whole prompt.

Quick Answers

What is the difference between Nano Banana, Nano Banana 2 and Nano Banana Pro?
The original Nano Banana is Gemini 2.5 Flash Image — the fast, fun model behind the viral figurine trend. Nano Banana 2 (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image) is the everyday model free Gemini accounts use. Nano Banana Pro is Gemini 3 Pro Image, the higher-fidelity model for complex scenes, dense text and multi-person consistency.

Is Nano Banana Pro free?
Not directly. The free Gemini app uses Nano Banana 2; paid Google AI plans add a "Redo with Pro" option that re-runs an image with Nano Banana Pro. Free downloads are 1K; paid plans download at 2K.

Why does Gemini change my face?
Because it generates a new image from your upload instead of retouching the photo. Every prompt on this page starts with a keep-list — face, features, skin tone, expression — so the model knows what must not move.

Can I use these prompts in ChatGPT instead?
The photo-edit prompts work in ChatGPT too — attach the photo and paste the prompt. The text and infographic prompts also transfer, but Pro's text rendering is the reason prompts 5 to 7 are written for Gemini; results elsewhere will vary.
